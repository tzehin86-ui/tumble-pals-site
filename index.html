Privacy Policy — Tumble Pals (跳跳小動物)

Last updated: August 22, 2026

Thank you for playing Tumble Pals (the "Game"). We take your privacy seriously.

Summary, stated accurately: we do not ask for your name, email, phone number or location, and there are no accounts. We do use one randomly generated device identifier for the online features (section 1b), we store one number on your device for the age question (section 2b), and the Game uses third-party services (Google AdMob for ads, Google Firebase Analytics for usage measurement) that collect limited data of their own. Connecting to our server also exposes your IP address to it, which we use only for abuse protection (section 1c). Sections 1, 1a, 1b, 1c, 2b and 6 describe each of those in full.

1. Data We (the developer) Collect

No directly identifying information — but we do use one persistent identifier. Read both halves; the second one matters under COPPA.

The Game does not collect:

  • Your real name, email, phone number, or location;
  • Any account or login (there are none);
  • Any text you type about yourself — your leaderboard name is generated by the Game from a fixed word list. The Game has exactly two typing boxes, a four-character room code and the parental-gate answer; section 4 lists both.

The Game does, if and only if you use the online features, send a randomly generated persistent device identifier to our server. Under COPPA a persistent identifier counts as personal information from a child even when it is not tied to a name, so we describe it in full in section 1b rather than claiming we collect "nothing".

⚠️ Earlier versions of this policy said "no personally identifiable information" in this section while section 1b described transmitting exactly such an identifier. That was a contradiction and it has been corrected here.

"Online features" here means Live Battle and the Daily Challenge. Please note that playing the Daily Challenge is itself an online feature: when a Daily Challenge run ends, your score is submitted automatically — you do not have to open the leaderboard, and there is no separate "submit" button. Only if you never play the Daily Challenge and never open Live Battle is no identifier created or sent.

1a. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The Game shows ads via Google AdMob. There are two kinds, and we list both because a child-directed app should not describe only the pleasant one:

  • Rewarded videos — always optional. You choose to watch one in exchange for something (extra coins, a revive, gems). Nothing in the Game requires it.
  • Interstitials — full-screen ads you did not ask for. These appear between runs. They are deliberately limited: never during the first 3 runs or first 3 minutes of a session, never right after a new personal best, at most one every 90 seconds, roughly one every 4 deaths, at most 3 per play session, and at most 4 in a day. The last two are hard ceilings — once reached, no further full-screen ad is shown no matter how long you keep playing. The daily count is stored on your device and survives closing or force-quitting the Game, so reopening it does not hand out a fresh batch of full-screen ads.

Rewarded videos have two daily ceilings. The first one covers the highest-frequency prompts: at most 12 rewarded videos a day. Once that is reached, the highest-frequency prompt (the "double this run's coins" offer that appears after a run ends) stops being offered for the rest of the day. The rest — ad revive, the daily gem video, the piggy-bank videos — keep working, because those are things you deliberately go and get, not interruptions. The second ceiling is absolute: at most 20 rewarded videos in a day across every placement, ad revive and the daily gem and piggy-bank videos included. Once that one is reached, no rewarded video is offered anywhere for the rest of the day. The terms of use, section 6, states the same two numbers.

Rewarded videos are also capped per play session, not only per day: the "double this run's coins" prompt stops after 4 in one sitting, and 8 in one sitting is an absolute ceiling across every placement. A daily cap alone does not stop a long single sitting from being dense with ads, and density in one sitting is what a child actually experiences. Together with the interstitial limits, one sitting can show at most 11 full-screen ads.

To serve ads, AdMob may collect and process limited data such as device identifiers, coarse location, and ad interactions. See Google's Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Which ads you see depends on the age you told us (section 2b):

  • If you are under 13, or if you have not answered the age question at all, AdMob's child-directed and under-age-of-consent settings are enabled: only non-personalized ads are served, no advertising identifier is used, and no install-attribution data is collected. This is also what happens if anything at all goes wrong reading your answer — the safe branch is the default.
  • If you are 13 or older, ads may be personalized, and Google's advertising identifier may be used. Between 13 and 15 the under-age-of-consent setting stays on, because in parts of the EEA the age of consent for data processing is 16.
  • Either way the Game only ever requests G-rated ad content, and Google Analytics ad-personalization and ad-user-data signals stay switched off in the app manifest for all users.

1b. Online features (Live Battle & Daily Challenge)

The Game sends the following to our matchmaking server when you use Live Battle, when you open the Daily Challenge leaderboard, and — automatically — at the end of every Daily Challenge run you play:

  • A random device identifier generated by the Game (not your device's advertising ID, not tied to any account) plus a signature that proves it is yours;
  • Your generated display name (e.g. "BraveCat42" — picked by the Game from a fixed word list; you cannot type your own text);
  • Your selected character, trail and prestige level (so they can be shown next to your name on the board);
  • Your score (floors reached) and duel rating.

This is the minimum needed to match you with an opponent and to rank you. We store it on the server so your rank persists between sessions.

The Daily Challenge submission is automatic, and stated plainly: finishing a Daily Challenge run sends your score, generated name, chosen character, trail and prestige level without asking you first. Opening the leaderboard is not what triggers it. If you have no connection at the time, the entry is saved on your device in a small queue and sent the next time the Game can reach our server — so a run played on a plane or underground is not lost, but it is also not discarded: it is transmitted later. Deleting the Game clears that queue along with everything else on the device.

How long entries are kept. The server keeps the seven most recent days that anyone actually submitted a score on — not the last seven calendar days. In practice these are nearly the same while the Game has daily players, but they are not the same promise: if a day passes with no submissions at all, it does not count towards the seven, so an entry from a quiet day can remain on the server for longer than seven calendar days. We would rather describe the rule the server actually applies than round it to a nicer number. Ladder rank persists until you stop playing.

We do not store your real name, email, contacts, precise location, or any free-typed text apart from the four-character room code described in section 4.

Removal. A daily leaderboard entry disappears on its own once seven newer submission days have accumulated, so one way to remove yours is to stop submitting — which means stopping playing the Daily Challenge, not merely avoiding the leaderboard screen — and wait. The self-service delete button described below is faster: it removes your ladder record and your daily leaderboard entries in one go.

For the persistent ladder entry, here is exactly what we can and cannot do, with no softening:

  • There is a self-service delete button, in the game: Duel lobby → "Delete my leaderboard record". It asks you to confirm, then deletes the row on the spot and shows you a receipt. No email, no waiting.
  • It authenticates you with the signed device identifier your app already holds, so it can only ever delete your own row — not someone else's, even if you both happen to have been given the same generated display name.
  • If the servers are unreachable you will be told the deletion did not happen, rather than being shown a success message that isn't true.
  • Deleting the Game stops all submissions and discards the identifier from your device. After that the row is inert: it can never be updated again, and it carries no name, email or location.
  • If you still want the row itself gone, email us (section 8) with your generated display name and roughly when you last played. We will look for it by hand and delete it if we can identify it, within 30 days. If we cannot identify it, we will tell you so plainly rather than pretend we did it.

We would rather write this down than promise a deletion tool we have not built.

If you never play the Daily Challenge and never open Live Battle, the Game sends nothing to our servers.

1c. IP addresses (server logs)

Any time the Game connects to our matchmaking server — which, as section 1b explains, includes finishing a Daily Challenge run — that connection carries your device's IP address, as every internet connection does. We are naming it here because in the EU an IP address counts as personal data even though we never link it to a name.

  • What we do with it. It is used only for abuse protection and rate limiting: capping how many simultaneous connections come from one address, and capping how many new player identifiers one address can create (a short-window limit and a rolling 24-hour limit). Without those caps a single script can exhaust the server for everyone.
  • Where it appears. The counters live in the server's memory and are discarded as the time windows expire. The address is also written to our server log when a connection is refused by one of those limits, so we can tell a genuine outage apart from an attack.
  • What we do not do with it. It is not stored in your leaderboard or ladder record, not sent to AdMob or Firebase, not used to build a profile of you, not used to derive your location beyond what an IP inherently implies, and never shown to other players.
  • How long. Server logs are kept for no more than 30 days and are then discarded. The in-memory rate-limit counters are gone within 24 hours or when the server restarts, whichever is sooner.

2. Local Storage

The Game stores the following on your device (via the operating system's local storage). Except for what section 1b describes, it stays on your device:

  • Your high scores and leaderboard entries;
  • Unlocked achievements and cumulative stats (coins, runs, etc.);
  • Your settings (character, control mode, sound, haptics);
  • A record of which in-app purchases have already been delivered to you (see section 5).

Deleting the Game removes all of this data from the device itself. However, on Android the Game opts in to the operating system's automatic backup, so a copy of that same data (progress, settings, and the record of delivered purchases) may also exist in your own Google Drive backup, and will be restored if you reinstall the Game or set up a new device. We keep this on deliberately: consumable purchases such as gem packs cannot be restored by the store, so without the backup a reinstall would silently destroy things you paid for. The backup belongs to your Google account, not to us — we cannot read it. To remove it, delete the Game's backup from your Google account's backup settings, or turn off backup for this app before uninstalling.

2a. Reminders (local notifications)

The Game can remind you what is waiting for you (for example, the size of tomorrow's daily login reward).

  • It is a local notification, not a push. It is scheduled by the app on your own device. There is no push token and no notification server, and the content of a reminder — what it says, and when it is set for — never leaves your device.
  • But the fact that you use reminders is counted. To be specific rather than reassuring: the Game sends an anonymous usage event named notify to Firebase Analytics (section 6) carrying a single field, action, whose value is one of enabled, disabled, permission_granted, permission_denied or scheduled. In plain terms we can see that you switched reminders on or off, that the system permission prompt was allowed or refused, and that a reminder was scheduled — and nothing more. No reminder text, no reminder time, no device identity beyond the app-instance identifier every Firebase Analytics event already carries. We use it to tell "nobody wants reminders" apart from "the permission prompt is being refused".
  • At most one reminder per calendar day, and at most three in total per round. Each time you play, the app replaces the queue with up to three reminders, and never more:
    1. tomorrow — what the next daily reward will be;
    2. before a streak runs out — only if you actually have a daily streak that is about to break;
    3. one week later — a single "come back" reminder.
    If two of those would land on the same day, only the earlier one is kept.
  • Then it goes quiet, permanently. The only way another round is ever scheduled is if you open the Game again. If you stop playing, you receive at most those three and then nothing further, ever — there is no fourth wave, no server push, and no repeating alarm.

⚠️ An earlier version of this policy said you would receive "exactly one" reminder and that there was "no second or third nudge". That was wrong: the app has always scheduled up to three. The description above is what the app actually does.

  • It never advertises. Reminders only mention rewards you have already earned or that are free to claim. They never mention gems, packs, prices, sales or anything purchasable.
  • It is quiet at night. Reminders are clamped to daytime hours (they are never delivered between 8pm and 9am local time).
  • Reminders are off until a grown-up turns them on. They are off by default on a fresh install, and the Game asks you once — inside the Game, after you have claimed a daily reward, never on first launch. Nothing is scheduled until that question is answered yes.

⚠️ An earlier version of the Game had reminders on by default and relied on the operating system's permission prompt as the opt-in. That prompt only exists on Android 13+ and on iOS — on Android 12 and older there is no runtime permission, so nobody had actually agreed. That is fixed: the in-game question is asked on every platform, and it is the only thing that turns reminders on.

  • You can turn it off again with the bell icon in the in-game settings, and via your device's own notification settings.

2b. The age question

At the first moment the answer actually matters, the Game asks one question: the year you were born. That is the whole age screen. Those moments are: your first real-money purchase, the first time a parental check opens, the first time a full-screen ad is about to play, and the first time the Game is about to show the store's own rating prompt. Just playing never triggers it.

  • We ask for the year only — never the full date of birth. A full birth date is stronger personal data than we need; the only thing we actually need to know is which advertising rules to apply to you.
  • The answer stays on your device. It is one number in the Game's local storage. It is never sent to us, never sent to AdMob, and never attached to your leaderboard entry.
  • The question is neutral. It does not pre-select a year, and it does not tell you what happens if you answer one way or another — because a screen that hints at the "better" answer is a screen that teaches children to lie to it.
  • If you skip it, or if the answer cannot be read back, the Game treats you as a child and applies the stricter advertising settings (section 1a). The safe branch is always the default.
  • "I don't know" is asked again once, after 3 days. If you answered with a birth year, the Game never asks again — re-asking someone who already answered would be asking until the answer changes. Only the "I don't know" branch comes back, and only after 3 days. For the whole time in between you stay on the strict, child-directed branch, and the repeat question is the same neutral screen with the same adult check.
  • Because the Game asks, it is a mixed-audience app rather than a child-directed-only one. Children get child-directed treatment; adults do not. Nothing about the gameplay, the prices, or what you can unlock changes based on your answer.

3. Device Permissions

  • Motion sensor (accelerometer): used solely for the optional "tilt control" gameplay, processed live on-device. It is never recorded or uploaded.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS on Android 13+): used solely for the reminders described in section 2a (at most one a day, at most three per round). Declining it does not limit the game in any way.
  • The Game does not request camera, microphone, contacts, location, or storage permissions.

4. Children's Privacy

The Game is suitable for all ages and is a mixed-audience app: it asks a neutral age question once — and once more after 3 days if that answer was "I don't know" (section 2b) — and then applies the stricter, child-directed advertising rules to everyone who is under 13 — and to everyone who has not answered, or whose answer cannot be read.

For those users: AdMob is configured as child-directed and under-age-of-consent, so only non-personalized ads are served, no advertising identifier is used, and no install attribution is collected — consistent with COPPA and GDPR-K. Firebase Analytics ad-personalization and ad-user-data signals are switched off in the app manifest for all users, adults included.

Beyond the device identifier described in sections 1 and 1b, the IP address described in section 1c, and the single number described in section 2b, we (the developer) collect no personal data. There are no accounts and no name or email fields.

Typing in the Game — the complete list. There are exactly two places where you can type anything at all, and neither of them accepts a name, an email address or a free message:

  1. The room code box in the Live Battle lobby. It takes a four-character code for joining a friend's room, and that code is sent to our matchmaking server — that is what it is for. It is a code for a room, not text about you.
  2. The parental gate answer box (the arithmetic question shown before any real-money purchase, section 5). What you type there is compared with the correct answer on your device only; the number you typed is never transmitted anywhere. Firebase Analytics does record whether a gate was passed and how many attempts it took (section 6) — but not the digits you entered.

An earlier version of this section said there is "no free-text input anywhere in the Game". That was wrong on its face — the two boxes above have always existed — and it is corrected here.

⚠️ An earlier version of this section said "We (the developer) collect no personal data" while section 1 described transmitting a persistent device identifier. That was a contradiction, and it is corrected above. An earlier version also said "there is no age screen, and no user is ever asked their age" — that is no longer true; see section 2b.

Real-money purchases are listed in full in section 5 and are behind a parental gate.

Any single spend that would use more than 250 purchased gems shows the parental gate. A grown-up can also switch on "gem spending needs a grown-up" in settings, which gates every gem spend; that switch is itself behind the parental gate.

  • Purchases are processed by the Apple App Store / Google Play. We never receive or store your payment details (e.g. card numbers). To disable purchases, use your device's parental controls / "Ask to Buy" / "Require authentication".
  • We keep a record on your device of which items you have received, so a purchase is not delivered twice. It contains no personal data.

6. Third Parties

  • Google AdMob (advertising) — see section 1a.
  • Google Firebase Analytics (usage measurement) — collects an app-instance identifier plus gameplay and commerce events. To be specific rather than vague, these include: how deep a run went and how it ended; whether a rewarded video was watched or was unavailable; coin and gem amounts earned and spent; which in-app purchase was made, at what price and in what currency; advertising revenue reported by AdMob for an individual ad impression; whether a parental gate was passed, per product; and whether daily reminders were switched on or off, whether the notification permission was granted or refused, and that a reminder was scheduled (section 2a). They are keyed to the app-instance identifier, not to a name, email or account. Ad-personalization and ad-user-data signals are switched off in the app manifest for all users.
  • The Android AD_ID permission is declared (it was previously removed). This is a consequence of the Game being mixed-audience: adults may receive personalized ads and install attribution. It is never used for users on the child-directed branch — for them the AdMob SDK is told not to collect or transmit it (section 1a), and that includes anyone who has not answered the age question.
  • Otherwise built with open-source software (Flutter, Flame, etc.); no other data-collecting third-party services.
